Twenty years of evidence, in five moments

The glass cliff is a research finding, not a slogan. It is also a live debate, and we cover both sides.

Explore the Research Library

  1. 2005

    Ryan and Haslam publish the FTSE 100 study and name the glass cliff.

  2. 2010

    Evidence from UK politics and the 'signal of change' experiments.

  3. 2013–14

    Cook and Glass extend the pattern to minority leaders and describe the saviour effect.

  4. 2020

    Meta-analysis in Psychological Bulletin finds a real but context-dependent effect.

  5. 2025

    New studies on UK chairs, US CEO selection, CEO dismissal risk and ethnic minority leaders.

Frequently asked questions

What is Glasscliff.com?

An independent guide for leaders taking on high-stakes roles, and for the boards that appoint them. We explain the glass cliff research, provide free tools to assess and negotiate risky roles, and connect people with experienced advisers.

Is Glasscliff.com affiliated with the researchers who coined the term?

No. 'Glass cliff' is a descriptive academic term coined by Michelle Ryan and Alex Haslam. We cite their work and others' but are not affiliated with them or their universities.

Are the tools free?

Yes. All five tools are free, run in your browser and do not send your answers anywhere unless you choose to submit a form.

What is a confidential role review?

You describe the role you are considering or holding, and we match you with an adviser (an executive coach, employment lawyer or turnaround specialist) who offers an initial conversation. Advisers set their own fees, which they agree with you directly.

How do you make money?

Through advertising (Google AdSense), sponsorships, the Advisor Network, referral fees from advisers, and support from readers. Sponsors never influence our editorial content.
